How to look round it

Phone or laptop, whichever you find easier. Both work.

  1. The first page

    The big photograph changes by itself. Scroll down slowly — the rooms, the food, the village, things to do nearby.

  2. The menu

    Three small lines at the top left. Tap them and ten pages slide down. Tap the lines again to close it.

  3. The villa tour — start here

    Thirty-five photographs you walk through one at a time. Swipe, or do nothing and it moves on its own. This is the part no other villa near you has.

    Open the tour

  4. The gallery

    Nine boxes, one for each part of the house. Tap a box to see the photographs full size, then swipe.

    Open the gallery

  5. The chat — please test this one

    The round button at the bottom right of every page. Type a question the way you would say it out loud.

    how many people can stay is there hot water how far is the airport

    Then ask it something it cannot know — how much does it cost. It should tell you it does not know and give your email. It should never invent a price.

    That is the part worth your time. A guest trusts a website that admits what it does not know.

  6. The journal

    Four pieces about Goa — the food, the old houses of Loutolim and Chandor, and why the monsoon is worth coming for.

    Open the journal

  7. The questions page

    Seventeen questions guests ask often. Tap one and the answer opens underneath.

    Open the questions

  8. The letters

    Put your own email in and send it. A welcome note should arrive within a minute — do check your spam folder and tell me if it lands there.

Two things I am not happy with

Better you hear them from me.

The photographs are small. They hold up on a phone, but on a laptop screen several of them break up, and no amount of processing fixes a picture that was not big enough to start with. If the photographer still has the original files, they would lift every page at once. A short video walk through the house would do even more.

The address is temporary. Everything sits at ikshaa.pages.dev. A proper domain — ikshaa.com or similar — costs very little a year, and it matters: Google builds up its opinion of a site against its domain and starts again from nothing when you move. The sooner that is settled, the sooner all of this starts counting.

What I need from you

The site says "ask the owner" wherever I could not confirm something. Every answer turns one of those into a real answer.

  • RatesWeekday, weekend, peak — or just tell me "the listing only", which is a fine answer
  • Minimum stayAnd whether it changes at Christmas
  • Cancellation and depositWhat happens, by when, and how much is refundable
  • How guests can payTransfer, card, UPI, cash on arrival
  • Pets, smoking, parties, childrenThe four that decide a booking
  • A phone or WhatsApp numberThe site only has your email. Most enquiries here start on WhatsApp
  • Cameras, and who is on the property overnightGuests ask, and they deserve a straight answer rather than silence
  • Parking, hot water, mosquito netsSmall things, asked constantly

One to double-check. I have taken the room numbers from your Airbnb listing — three bedrooms, four beds, two bathrooms, up to nine guests, check-in from 2pm, check-out by 11am.

The website now states those as fact. Please check them against what you would tell a guest on the phone, because if the listing is out of date anywhere, the website is too.

If anything looks wrong or reads oddly, just tell me two things: which page you were on, and what you saw. A photograph in the wrong place, a word spelled wrong, an answer that sounds strange — all of it is quick to change.

Nothing is too small to mention.
